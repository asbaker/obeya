# Hifi: synthesize

The finished piece: what gets presented, signed off or built exactly as drawn. Work as a professional designer the person hired: you make the calls for this content, this audience and this page, and you can say why. `--style hifi` sets nothing; every choice is yours. Read `--skill design` first; all of it applies at full strength.

## Turn it up

Hifi is not a tidier clean. Clean is the structure in greys; hifi is the thing itself, with a point of view:

- **Real content everywhere:** names, numbers, dates, copy as the product would say it. No lorem, "Label" or "Button".
- **A visual language:** a palette with roles, a type scale, a surface system, one icon family, emphasis that's earned.
- **Bold where the content earns it:** a huge number, an editorial headline, a full-bleed colour field, a dark title band, a chart instead of a table. Restraint is a choice too, not the default.
- **Specific enough to ship:** someone could build, print or present it without redrawing.

## The process

1. **Read the content.** What's the story, what's the one number, what's the order someone reads it in?
2. **Find the look** (`--skill design`): the page's, the person's, or one you set. On a page with a consistent look, hifi means that look at its best, not a new one.
3. **Choose the voice:** calm product, warm editorial, dense technical, bold marketing. It picks the fonts, the palette and how much space there is.
4. **Sample first:** draw one card, the title band and one chart where the first frame will go; `shot` it; adjust; then build the frames around it.
5. **Draw the main view, then what it needs:** the next screen, the states (empty, loading, error, the long name, the zero) when they matter.
6. **Finish:** squint at the `shot`. Does the eye land on the focal point first? Every edge lines up? Then remove one thing.

## Type

- Two families, three at most (one may be mono for eyebrows or numbers). Pairings that work: `Nunito` titles + `Helvetica` body + `Cascadia` eyebrows; `Source Serif` headlines + `Inter`; `Inter Bold` + `Inter`; `Barlow Condensed` for numbers.
- A scale from the frame's width (`--skill design`), in a few steps. The key number big; units small and muted.
- Eyebrow, title, one line of why: a header that does work. Left-align text; centre only short labels; lines under ~60 characters.

## Colour

- Roles, not swatches: background, surface, ink, muted ink, hairline, then a primary, a secondary, and status colours used only for status.
- Take colour from the subject or the brand; tints for areas, saturated colour on small things; dark surfaces for emphasis when the page can carry them.
- Categories get a legend; contrast stays 4.5:1 for text.

## Space and shape

- A grid with generous margins; the gap between groups at least twice the gap inside one; one corner radius everywhere.
- Fewer boxes: group with space and a surface tint; borders as hairlines.
- A stripe on a rounded card goes inside it as a pill; a square stripe pokes out of the corners.
- Full-frame backgrounds are locked by the server, so the person doesn't drag them.

## Slides and boards

A slide takes the screen's shape and holds one idea, its takeaway as the title. A board that tells a story (a plan, a spec, a review) is as tall as it needs, read top to bottom in sections with eyebrows.

## Watch for

Rainbow colour without meaning, emoji as icons, centred paragraphs, boxes inside boxes, heavy outlines, more than three sizes in one block, placeholder text, text sized for a laptop on a 3,000-px frame. Break any of these when you can say why.

## Examples, not targets

Good hifi has looked like a product gallery (a brand mark, a launch page, a dashboard, an architecture diagram), and like a plan board: eyebrows in mono, Nunito titles, white cards with an inset stripe, a coral critical chain, pink numbered pins for questions. Learn from them; don't copy them onto content they don't fit.
